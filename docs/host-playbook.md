# Host playbook

Hosting rotates, so the process has to live somewhere other than in one person's
head. This is that place. Pin it in the group.

The point of all this: **if something goes badly wrong, what protects you is
having behaved reasonably and being able to show it.** Documents help. Records
help more.

**Start from this: you host in your own name.** There is no entity standing
behind you. The session you post is yours — your venue, your player list, your
cost per head, your call on refunds and on who sits out — and
[the terms](../terms.html) say exactly that. Being an admin of the group is not
hosting; only being named as the host of a session is. So name yourself in every
game post, and never post a game as though "the club" is running it.

---

## Before the session

- [ ] Court booked and **paid for in writing** — keep the receipt / UPI
      confirmation / booking message.
- [ ] Game posted with the player list and the standard consent footer
      (see [whatsapp-consent.md](whatsapp-consent.md)).
- [ ] Anyone new on the list has replied "agree" to the pinned message.
- [ ] First-aid kit is coming with you (see contents below).

## On arrival — the court sweep

Two minutes, every time. Do it before anyone starts warming up.

This is a player's look-over, not a safety inspection — you aren't qualified to
certify a court and the terms don't claim you are. Do it anyway: it's how you
catch the obvious stuff, and noticing the obvious stuff is the one safety thing
players are told to expect from you.

- [ ] **Surface** — wet patches, sand over concrete, cracks, potholes, loose tiles.
- [ ] **Debris** — glass, stones, screws, anything sharp.
- [ ] **Net and posts** — posts stable and padded, guy ropes not a trip hazard,
      no protruding hooks or cleats.
- [ ] **Run-off space** — nothing hard within the run-out area: benches, bags,
      poles, low walls.
- [ ] **Lighting** — good enough for evening games; check before dusk, not after.
- [ ] **Water** — drinking water available or players have their own.
- [ ] **Exit route** — know how an ambulance would get in.

**If something is unsafe: report it to the venue and don't play on it. Do not fix
or modify venue equipment yourself** — repairing it makes its condition your
responsibility instead of theirs.

The same applies mid-session. If an obvious serious hazard turns up once you've
started — glass on the sand, a post that's come loose, lights failing — stop
play and tell the venue. Playing on because the court's paid for is the decision
that's hard to defend later.

## During play

- No one plays visibly intoxicated. Ask them to sit out; you don't owe an
  argument.
- No one plays who's clearly injured or unwell.
- Under-18s can't play. See [whatsapp-consent.md](whatsapp-consent.md) for why.
- Don't coach, and don't let the session be described as coaching or training —
  presenting yourself as an instructor raises the standard of care you're held to.
- Step in early on aggressive play or conduct problems. You're allowed to end
  someone's session.

## If someone is injured

1. **Stop play.**
2. **Don't move them** if there's any chance of head, neck or back injury.
3. **Call 108** for anything serious. Don't drive someone yourself if an
   ambulance is the right call.
4. **Stay with them** until help or family arrives.
5. **Don't give medical advice** and don't hand out medication — not even
   painkillers.
6. **Never leave the scene.** Under BNS s.106, causing death by negligence
   carries up to 7 years — but **up to 10 years if you leave or fail to report
   it**. Staying and reporting matters enormously.
7. **Write it up the same day** using
   [incident-report-template.md](incident-report-template.md), while memories are
   fresh and witnesses are reachable. Contemporaneous notes are worth far more
   later than a reconstruction months on.
8. Tell the other hosts. Don't handle a serious incident alone.

**Don't admit fault or promise to cover costs at the scene.** Be kind, be
helpful, stay with them — but "I'm so sorry, we'll pay for this" is an admission,
and it isn't yours alone to make. Help, then report it accurately.

## First-aid kit

Whoever hosts brings it; keep it stocked and hand it over at the end.

Crepe bandages · adhesive bandages · antiseptic (Betadine/Savlon) · sterile gauze
· medical tape · instant cold packs · scissors · disposable gloves · ORS sachets
· a saline eyewash · tape for fingers.

**No medication.** Not paracetamol, not painkillers, not sprays. Handing out
medicine is a liability you don't want and aren't qualified for.

Also know: the nearest hospital to each venue, and have it written down rather
than searched for in a panic.

## Money

Keep it visibly at-cost — that's what stops "splitting the court" from looking
like running a business.

- Collect **only** each player's share of that session's court fee. It's a
  pass-through: the venue's money passing through your account. No commission,
  no rounding up, no profit.
- **Say what your refund position is when you post.** It's your booking, so it's
  your call — but players should hear it before they pay, not after they drop
  out.
- **Keep the venue receipt** for every session.
- **Run no surplus.** If you over-collect, refund it or carry it visibly against
  the next booking. A growing balance in a personal account is the thing that
  turns cost-sharing into income.
- Log every session (below). Any player can ask to see it.

## Venue bookings

- Get the booking **in writing** — even a WhatsApp confirmation is far better
  than a phone call.
- Keep receipts.
- Ask the venue whether they carry public liability insurance. Their answer tells
  you a lot about who carries the risk for court conditions.
- **Read anything before you sign it.** With no entity behind us, a host who
  signs a venue agreement is binding *themselves personally* — including any
  indemnity clause buried in it. If a venue wants a signature on anything more
  than a receipt, get another host to look at it first.
